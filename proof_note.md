# Edge-motif WL beyond triangles: proof dossier and experimental targets

This note is meant to sit next to the EB-1WL manuscript.  It does **not** replace that manuscript.  It generalizes the proof template to 4-cycle edge message passing and isolates one major open problem:

> **Major open problem.**  Determine whether the strongest triangle-plus-4-cycle edge refinement, denoted here by \(\Delta+\square_1\)-EB-WL, is strictly weaker than full 2-WL, or whether it already has full 2-WL homomorphism expressivity on finite simple graphs.  Equivalently, characterize the quotient-closed rooted pattern class generated by edge incidence, triangle witnesses, and simple 4-cycle witnesses, and decide whether equality of homomorphism counts from that class is equivalent to equality of all treewidth-two homomorphism counts.

The reason this is the right open problem is that Barceló--Jogl--Kozachinskiy--Lanzinger--Neumann--Rojas introduce EB-1WL/EB-GNN as edge message passing through incidence and triangles, prove it is near-linear on sparse graphs, and prove a homomorphism lower bound from chordal treewidth-two graphs.  Böhm's thesis extends the architecture by adding 4-cycle aggregation and explicitly states that formally characterizing the combined triangle/4-cycle expressive power is a natural future direction.  The proof work below gives the formal finite-model-theoretic scaffold for that direction.

The note has four outputs.

1. A general edge-motif EF game.
2. A general rooted-pattern homomorphism theorem.
3. Specialization to triangle and 4-cycle EB refinements.
4. A concrete experimental search plan and code interface.

---

## 1. Discrete edge-motif refinements

All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and loopless.  For a graph \(G\), write

\[
\vec E(G)=\{(u,v):uv\in E(G)\}
\]

for the set of ordered edges.  An edge-motif refinement colors ordered edges.  At round \(t\), the color of \((u,v)\) is denoted

\[
\chi_t^G(u,v).
\]

Initially, all ordered edges have the same color:

\[
\chi_0^G(u,v)=0.
\]

Every mode considered below includes the old color and the two incidence multisets:

\[
\chi_t^G(u,v),
\qquad
\multiset{\chi_t^G(u,x):x\in N_G(u)},
\qquad
\multiset{\chi_t^G(v,z):z\in N_G(v)}.
\]

The optional motif terms are as follows.

### 1.1 Triangle term

The triangle term is

\[
T_t^G(u,v)
=
\multiset{(\chi_t^G(u,y),\chi_t^G(v,y)):y\in N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)}.
\]

This is exactly the triangle/common-neighbor term in EB-1WL.

### 1.2 One-type 4-cycle term

For an ordered edge \((u,v)\), a simple oriented 4-cycle witness is a pair \((y,w)\) such that

\[
u,y,w,v
\]

are pairwise distinct and

\[
uy,yw,wv,vu\in E(G).
\]

Chords \(uw\) and \(vy\) are allowed.  Thus the 4-cycle is not required to be induced.

The one-type 4-cycle term is

\[
Q_{1,t}^G(u,v)
=
\multiset{
(\chi_t^G(u,y),\chi_t^G(v,w),\chi_t^G(y,w))
:
(y,w)\text{ is a simple oriented 4-cycle witness for }(u,v)
}.
\]

This is the discrete color-refinement analogue of feeding the three edge features \((u,y),(v,w),(y,w)\) jointly into one aggregation channel.

### 1.3 Two-type 4-cycle term

The two-type 4-cycle term separates the two neighboring edges from the opposite edge:

\[
Q_{2,t}^{G,\mathrm{near}}(u,v)
=
\multiset{(\chi_t^G(u,y),\chi_t^G(v,w)):(y,w)\text{ is a 4-cycle witness for }(u,v)},
\]

and

\[
Q_{2,t}^{G,\mathrm{opp}}(u,v)
=
\multiset{\chi_t^G(y,w):(y,w)\text{ is a 4-cycle witness for }(u,v)}.
\]

This is the discrete analogue of the two-channel 4-cycle architecture in which the neighboring pair and the opposite edge are aggregated separately.

### 1.4 Modes

We use the following names.

\[
\Delta\text{-EB-WL}:\quad \text{old + incidence + triangle.}
\]

\[
\square_1\text{-EB-WL}:\quad \text{old + incidence + one-type 4-cycle.}
\]

\[
\square_2\text{-EB-WL}:\quad \text{old + incidence + two-type 4-cycle.}
\]

\[
\Delta+\square_1\text{-EB-WL}:\quad \text{old + incidence + triangle + one-type 4-cycle.}
\]

\[
\Delta+\square_2\text{-EB-WL}:\quad \text{old + incidence + triangle + two-type 4-cycle.}
\]

The graph-level signature is the vertex count together with the multiset of unordered pairs of stable colors of the two orientations of each undirected edge:

\[
\operatorname{Sig}(G)=
\left(
|V(G)|,
\multiset{\multiset{\chi_\infty^G(u,v),\chi_\infty^G(v,u)}:uv\in E(G)}
\right).
\]

Two graphs are equivalent for a mode if their signatures agree after running the refinement on their disjoint union.

## 2. EF games

For each mode \(M\), define an edge EF game \(\mathsf{EF}_r^M(G,H;(u,v),(u',v'))\).  A position is a pair of ordered edges, one in \(G\) and one in \(H\).  Duplicator wins every 0-round position.

At an \((r+1)\)-round position \(((u,v),(u',v'))\), Spoiler chooses a move type available in the mode.  Each move can be played starting in either graph.  We write the version where Spoiler starts in \(G\).

### 2.1 Stay move

Spoiler keeps the current ordered edge.  The next position is

\[
((u,v),(u',v')).
\]

This move tests the old color.

### 2.2 Left-incidence move

Duplicator chooses a bijection

\[
\beta_L:N_G(u)\to N_H(u').
\]

Spoiler chooses \(x\in N_G(u)\).  The next position is

\[
((u,x),(u',\beta_L(x))).
\]

### 2.3 Right-incidence move

Duplicator chooses a bijection

\[
\beta_R:N_G(v)\to N_H(v').
\]

Spoiler chooses \(z\in N_G(v)\).  The next position is

\[
((v,z),(v',\beta_R(z))).
\]

### 2.4 Triangle move

This move is available in modes containing \(\Delta\).  Duplicator chooses a bijection

\[
\beta_T:N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)\to N_H(u')\cap N_H(v').
\]

Spoiler chooses \(y\in N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)\), and then chooses one of the two wedge edges.  The next position is either

\[
((u,y),(u',\beta_T(y)))
\]

or

\[
((v,y),(v',\beta_T(y))).
\]

### 2.5 One-type square move

This move is available in modes containing \(\square_1\).  Let

\[
\operatorname{Sq}_G(u,v)=\{(y,w):u,y,w,v\text{ are pairwise distinct and }uy,yw,wv,vu\in E(G)\}.
\]

Duplicator chooses a bijection

\[
\beta_Q:\operatorname{Sq}_G(u,v)\to \operatorname{Sq}_H(u',v').
\]

Spoiler chooses \((y,w)\in\operatorname{Sq}_G(u,v)\), receives \((y',w')=\beta_Q(y,w)\), and then chooses one of three oriented edges:

\[
(u,y),\qquad (v,w),\qquad (y,w).
\]

The next position is the corresponding ordered-edge pair:

\[
((u,y),(u',y')),
\quad
((v,w),(v',w')),
\quad\text{or}\quad
((y,w),(y',w')).
\]

### 2.6 Two-type square moves

For \(\square_2\), there are two square moves.

The neighboring-edge move uses a bijection between square witnesses and allows Spoiler to choose either \((u,y)\) or \((v,w)\).  The opposite-edge move uses a bijection between square witnesses and forces the next position to \((y,w)\).  Equivalently, Duplicator must separately match the multiset of neighboring-edge color pairs and the multiset of opposite-edge colors.  This is exactly the game form of the two marginal 4-cycle aggregations.

### 2.7 Game-color theorem

**Theorem 2.1.**  For every mode \(M\), every \(r\ge 0\), and every pair of ordered edges \((u,v)\in\vec E(G)\), \((u',v')\in\vec E(H)\),

\[
\chi_r^G(u,v)=\chi_r^H(u',v')
\]

if and only if Duplicator wins

\[
\mathsf{EF}_r^M(G,H;(u,v),(u',v')).
\]

### Proof

The proof is by induction on \(r\).

For \(r=0\), every ordered edge has the same initial color, and Duplicator wins every 0-round position.

Assume the statement for \(r\).  Consider a position \(((u,v),(u',v'))\).

Suppose first that

\[
\chi_{r+1}^G(u,v)=\chi_{r+1}^H(u',v').
\]

The update tuple for \((u,v)\) equals the update tuple for \((u',v')\).  Therefore every multiset component in the update agrees with its counterpart in \(H\).

For the stay move, equality of the old-color component gives

\[
\chi_r^G(u,v)=\chi_r^H(u',v'),
\]

so Duplicator wins the remaining \(r\) rounds by the induction hypothesis.

For the left-incidence move, equality of the left multisets gives a color-preserving bijection

\[
\beta_L:N_G(u)\to N_H(u')
\]

such that

\[
\chi_r^G(u,x)=\chi_r^H(u',\beta_L(x))
\]

for every \(x\in N_G(u)\).  After Spoiler chooses \(x\), the induction hypothesis gives a winning continuation.  The right-incidence move is identical.

For the triangle move, equality of the triangle multisets gives a bijection

\[
\beta_T:N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)\to N_H(u')\cap N_H(v')
\]

such that

\[
(\chi_r^G(u,y),\chi_r^G(v,y))
=
(\chi_r^H(u',\beta_T(y)),\chi_r^H(v',\beta_T(y))).
\]

Whichever of the two wedge edges Spoiler chooses, the resulting ordered edges have equal \(r\)-colors.  Duplicator wins by induction.

For the one-type square move, equality of the square triple multisets gives a bijection

\[
\beta_Q:\operatorname{Sq}_G(u,v)\to \operatorname{Sq}_H(u',v')
\]

such that, for \((y',w')=\beta_Q(y,w)\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi_r^G(u,y)&=\chi_r^H(u',y'),\\
\chi_r^G(v,w)&=\chi_r^H(v',w'),\\
\chi_r^G(y,w)&=\chi_r^H(y',w').
\end{aligned}
\]

Spoiler then chooses one of these three components, and the induction hypothesis applies.

For the two-type square moves, equality of the two marginal square multisets gives the required bijections for the neighboring-edge pair move and the opposite-edge move.  Again, the chosen next edge has equal \(r\)-color.

Conversely, suppose

\[
\chi_{r+1}^G(u,v)\ne \chi_{r+1}^H(u',v').
\]

Then at least one component of the update tuple differs.  If the old-color component differs, Spoiler uses the stay move and wins by induction.  If a left or right incidence multiset differs, no bijection can preserve all \(r\)-colors, so Spoiler chooses a wrongly matched neighbor and wins by induction.  If the triangle-pair multiset differs, no bijection can preserve all pairs; Spoiler chooses a witness whose pair is mismatched and then chooses a mismatched component.  If a square triple multiset differs, no bijection can preserve all triples; Spoiler chooses a witness whose triple is mismatched and then chooses a mismatched component.  For \(\square_2\), the same argument applies to whichever marginal multiset differs.

This proves the theorem.

## 3. Rooted pattern constructors

A rooted oriented-edge pattern is a finite graph \(P\) together with an ordered root edge \(\rho=(a,b)\in\vec E(P)\).  For a host ordered edge \((u,v)\in\vec E(G)\), define

\[
h_P^G(u,v)=\#\{\varphi:P\to G:\varphi(a)=u,\;\varphi(b)=v\}.
\]

The following constructors mirror the update terms.

### 3.1 Base and root product

The base pattern is the single rooted edge \(K_2\).  It satisfies

\[
h_{K_2}^G(u,v)=1.
\]

If \(P,Q\) are rooted at \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\), their root product \(P\odot Q\) identifies \(a=c\) and \(b=d\).  Then

\[
h_{P\odot Q}^G(u,v)=h_P^G(u,v)h_Q^G(u,v).
\]

### 3.2 Incidence constructors

The left constructor \(L(P)\) creates a new root edge \((r,s)\) and glues the old root of \(P\) to \((r,x)\), where \(x\) is a new vertex adjacent to \(r\).  Then

\[
h_{L(P)}^G(u,v)=\sum_{x\in N_G(u)}h_P^G(u,x).
\]

The right constructor \(R(P)\) satisfies

\[
h_{R(P)}^G(u,v)=\sum_{z\in N_G(v)}h_P^G(v,z).
\]

### 3.3 Triangle constructor

Given rooted patterns \(P,Q\), the triangle constructor \(T(P,Q)\) creates a root edge \((r,s)\), a new vertex \(y\), the triangle edges \(rs,ry,sy\), glues \(P\) to \((r,y)\), and glues \(Q\) to \((s,y)\).  Then

\[
h_{T(P,Q)}^G(u,v)=\sum_{y\in N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)}h_P^G(u,y)h_Q^G(v,y).
\]

### 3.4 Walk-square constructor

The clean homomorphism-count constructor for 4-cycles is the walk-square constructor.  Given rooted patterns \(P,Q,R\), define \(W(P,Q,R)\) by creating a root edge \((r,s)\), two new vertices \(y,w\), edges

\[
rs,\quad ry,\quad yw,\quad ws,
\]

and gluing

\[
P\text{ to }(r,y),\qquad Q\text{ to }(s,w),\qquad R\text{ to }(y,w).
\]

Then

\[
h_{W(P,Q,R)}^G(u,v)
=
\sum_{y\in N_G(u),\;w\in N_G(v),\;yw\in E(G)}
h_P^G(u,y)h_Q^G(v,w)h_R^G(y,w).
\]

This identity is exact for the walk-square convention, where degeneracies are allowed.

### 3.5 Simple-square constructor and quotient closure

The implemented 4-cycle variants usually want simple 4-cycles: \(u,v,y,w\) pairwise distinct.  The corresponding count is

\[
\sum_{(y,w)\in \operatorname{Sq}_G(u,v)}
h_P^G(u,y)h_Q^G(v,w)h_R^G(y,w).
\]

This is a rooted **injective-gadget** count.  It is still controlled by ordinary homomorphism counts after quotient closure.

The principle is standard inclusion--exclusion over equality patterns.  For a fixed finite gadget \(A\) with boundary vertices fixed to \(u,v\), the number of assignments of the internal vertices satisfying specified inequalities is an integer linear combination of unrestricted assignments over quotient gadgets \(A/\pi\), where \(\pi\) ranges over partitions of the internal vertices and boundary vertices allowed by the inclusion--exclusion formula.  Quotients producing loops contribute zero on loopless host graphs.  Therefore every simple-square rooted count is an integer linear combination of rooted homomorphism counts from quotient patterns of walk-square constructors.

This quotient closure is the only technical difference between walk-square theory and simple 4-cycle theory.

## 4. General homomorphism theorem

Fix a mode \(M\).  Let \(\mathcal R_M\) be the smallest class of rooted oriented-edge patterns satisfying:

1. \(K_2\in\mathcal R_M\).
2. \(\mathcal R_M\) is closed under root product.
3. \(\mathcal R_M\) is closed under \(L\) and \(R\).
4. If \(M\) contains \(\Delta\), then \(\mathcal R_M\) is closed under \(T\).
5. If \(M\) contains \(\square_1\), then \(\mathcal R_M\) is closed under the square constructor \(W(P,Q,R)\), and, for simple-square semantics, under its equality-pattern quotients.
6. If \(M\) contains \(\square_2\), then \(\mathcal R_M\) is closed under the marginal square constructors corresponding to \((P,Q)\) on the neighboring pair and \(R\) on the opposite edge, again with quotient closure under simple-square semantics.

Let \(\mathcal C_M\) be the unrooted class obtained by closing two rooted patterns along opposite root orientations and also allowing isolated vertices:

\[
F=P\oplus_{\mathrm{opp}}Q,
\]

where the root \((a,b)\) of \(P\) is identified with the reverse \((d,c)\) of the root \((c,d)\) of \(Q\).

### Theorem 4.1: exact rooted span theorem

For every mode \(M\), every round \(t\), and every color class \(C\) of ordered edges after \(t\) rounds on a finite disjoint union of graphs, the indicator \(\mathbf 1_C(u,v)\) is a rational linear combination of rooted counts

\[
h_P(u,v),\qquad P\in\mathcal R_M.
\]

#### Proof

The proof is by induction on \(t\).

At \(t=0\), there is one ordered-edge color class, and its indicator is the constant function \(1\).  This is \(h_{K_2}\).

Assume the statement for round \(t\).  A round-\((t+1)\) color is a finite tuple of integer-valued coordinate functions:

* the previous color;
* counts of left-neighbor colors;
* counts of right-neighbor colors;
* if present, counts of triangle color pairs;
* if present, counts of square color triples or square marginals.

By the induction hypothesis, every old color indicator \(\mathbf 1_D(u,v)\) is a rational linear combination of rooted counts from \(\mathcal R_M\).

For a left-neighbor count,

\[
\sum_{x\in N(u)}\mathbf 1_D(u,x),
\]

expand \(\mathbf 1_D(u,x)\) as a rational linear combination of \(h_P(u,x)\), then apply the identity for \(L(P)\).  Thus the left-neighbor coordinate lies in the rational span of \(\mathcal R_M\).  The right-neighbor coordinate is identical.

For a triangle-pair count,

\[
\sum_{y\in N(u)\cap N(v)}\mathbf 1_{D_1}(u,y)\mathbf 1_{D_2}(v,y),
\]

expand both indicators.  Products of rooted counts are represented by root products inside the summand, and the outer sum over common neighbors is represented by \(T\).  Hence the coordinate lies in the span.

For a one-type square-triple count,

\[
\sum_{(y,w)\in\operatorname{Sq}(u,v)}
\mathbf 1_{D_1}(u,y)
\mathbf 1_{D_2}(v,w)
\mathbf 1_{D_3}(y,w),
\]

expand the three indicators.  Each product term is represented by the square constructor, and, under simple-square semantics, by the quotient-closed square constructor via inclusion--exclusion.  Thus the coordinate lies in the span.  For the two-type square marginals, the same argument applies to the two corresponding marginal sums.

So every coordinate function defining the new color is in the rational algebra generated by rooted counts in \(\mathcal R_M\).  A color class is a fiber of a finite tuple of such coordinates over a finite domain.  The indicator of a fiber is a rational polynomial in the coordinate functions by Lagrange interpolation:

\[
\mathbf 1_{F_1=a_1,\ldots,F_s=a_s}
=
\prod_{i=1}^s\prod_{b\in F_i(\Omega)\setminus\{a_i\}}\frac{F_i-b}{a_i-b}.
\]

The algebra is closed under products because root product represents multiplication.  Therefore the new color-class indicator is in the rational span of rooted counts from \(\mathcal R_M\).

### Theorem 4.2: exact unrooted separation theorem

If two graphs \(G,H\) are distinguished by mode \(M\), then there exists \(F\in\mathcal C_M\) such that

\[
\hom(F,G)\ne\hom(F,H).
\]

Conversely, every \(F\in\mathcal C_M\) has its homomorphism count determined by a sufficiently deep stable \(M\)-color signature.  Hence

\[
G\equiv_M H
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\hom(F,G)=\hom(F,H)\text{ for every }F\in\mathcal C_M.
\]

#### Proof: distinguishability implies a hom witness

If \(|V(G)|\ne |V(H)|\), take \(F=K_1\).

Otherwise, a signature difference means that for stable ordered-edge colors \(c,d\),

\[
S_{c,d}(G)=\sum_{(u,v)\in\vec E(G)}\mathbf 1_c(u,v)\mathbf 1_d(v,u)
\]

differs from \(S_{c,d}(H)\).

By Theorem 4.1,

\[
\mathbf 1_c(u,v)=\sum_i\alpha_i h_{P_i}(u,v),
\qquad
\mathbf 1_d(v,u)=\sum_j\beta_j h_{Q_j}(v,u)
\]

for rooted patterns \(P_i,Q_j\in\mathcal R_M\).  Therefore

\[
S_{c,d}(G)=
\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\beta_j
\sum_{(u,v)\in\vec E(G)}h_{P_i}(u,v)h_{Q_j}(v,u).
\]

For each \((i,j)\), the inner sum is exactly

\[
\hom(P_i\oplus_{\mathrm{opp}}Q_j,G).
\]

The same expansion holds for \(H\).  Since the rational linear combination of differences is nonzero, at least one term is nonzero.  That term gives the required unrooted witness.

#### Proof: hom counts from \(\mathcal C_M\) are determined by colors

It suffices to prove that every rooted count \(h_P(u,v)\), \(P\in\mathcal R_M\), is determined by a sufficiently deep color of \((u,v)\).  This is another induction over the construction of \(P\).

For \(K_2\), the count is always \(1\).

For root product, the count is the product of two previously determined counts.

For \(L\), \(R\), \(T\), and \(W\), the update contains exactly the multiset needed to sum the previously determined rooted counts over the relevant witness set.  For simple squares, the quotient-closed/inclusion--exclusion expansion reduces the injective witness count to finitely many unrestricted rooted counts, each handled by the induction.

Finally, an unrooted witness \(F=P\oplus_{\mathrm{opp}}Q\) is counted by

\[
\hom(F,G)=\sum_{(u,v)\in\vec E(G)}h_P(u,v)h_Q(v,u),
\]

and the graph signature gives the multiset of stable colors of opposite orientations.  Therefore it determines the sum.

## 5. Structural consequences

### 5.1 Triangle-only recovers the EB-1WL manuscript

For \(M=\Delta\), the class \(\mathcal C_M\) is exactly the chordal graphs of treewidth at most two, with isolated vertices allowed.  This is the theorem proved in the main manuscript.

### 5.2 Square constructors have treewidth at most two

The square gadget \(r-y-w-s-r\) has treewidth two.  The constructors glue rooted patterns along edges, and clique-sums along edges preserve treewidth at most two.  Equality-pattern quotients of treewidth-two graphs also have treewidth at most two because contracting vertices cannot increase treewidth.  Hence

\[
\mathcal C_{\Delta+\square_1}\subseteq\{F:\operatorname{tw}(F)\le 2\}.
\]

Therefore full 2-WL is at least as expressive as \(\Delta+\square_1\)-EB-WL and \(\Delta+\square_2\)-EB-WL.

### 5.3 One-type square dominates two-type square

The one-type square update sees the full multiset

\[
\multiset{(a,b,c)}.
\]

The two-type square update sees only the two marginals

\[
\multiset{(a,b)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\multiset{c}.
\]

The full triple multiset determines both marginals.  Therefore, by induction on rounds,

\[
\square_2\preceq \square_1
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Delta+\square_2\preceq \Delta+\square_1.
\]

Whether either containment is strict is open.  The experimental code searches for finite graph pairs witnessing strictness.

### 5.4 Triangle and square-only refinements are incomparable

Let

\[
G=C_3\sqcup C_3,
\qquad
H=C_6.
\]

Both graphs are 2-regular with six vertices and six edges.  Under square-only refinement, every directed edge in both graphs has the same old color, the same left/right incidence profile, and no simple 4-cycle witnesses.  By induction all directed edges in \(G\sqcup H\) keep the same color, so square-only refinement does not distinguish \(G,H\).

Triangle-only refinement distinguishes them after one round: every directed edge of \(C_3\sqcup C_3\) has one common neighbor with its reverse endpoint, while every directed edge of \(C_6\) has none.

Conversely, let

\[
G'=C_4\sqcup C_4,
\qquad
H'=C_8.
\]

Both graphs are 2-regular with eight vertices and eight edges, and both are triangle-free.  Triangle-only refinement does not distinguish them: all directed edges have the same old color, the same left/right incidence profile, and empty triangle multiset at every round.  Square-only refinement distinguishes them after one round: every directed edge of \(C_4\sqcup C_4\) has a simple 4-cycle witness, while no directed edge of \(C_8\) has one.

Therefore

\[
\Delta\parallel\square_1
\]

and similarly

\[
\Delta\parallel\square_2.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\Delta\prec \Delta+\square_i
\quad\text{and}\quad
\square_i\prec \Delta+\square_i
\]

for \(i\in\{1,2\}\).

## 6. The main open problem

The exact theorem above turns the informal expressivity question for 4-cycle EB-GNNs into a finite-model-theoretic graph-class problem.

Let

\[
\mathcal C_{\Delta\square_1}
\]

be the quotient-closed unrooted class generated by incidence, triangle, and one-type simple-square constructors.

We know

\[
\mathcal C_{\Delta}
\subsetneq
\mathcal C_{\Delta\square_2}
\subseteq
\mathcal C_{\Delta\square_1}
\subseteq
\{F:\operatorname{tw}(F)\le 2\}.
\]

The major open problem is to decide the rightmost containment:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{\Delta\square_1}uildrel ?\over=\{F:\operatorname{tw}(F)\le 2\}
}
\]

or, equivalently, whether \(\Delta+\square_1\)-EB-WL is as expressive as full 2-WL.

A strict separation would be a pair \(G,H\) such that

\[
G\equiv_{\Delta+\square_1}H
\quad\text{but}\quad
G\not\equiv_{2\mathrm{WL}}H.
\]

An equality proof would need to show that the recursively generated quotient-closed class contains enough patterns to recover every treewidth-two homomorphism count.

The same framework isolates the secondary open problem

\[
\boxed{
\Delta+\square_2uildrel ?\over=\Delta+\square_1
}
\]

at the level of exact color refinement.  Böhm observed empirically that one-type and two-type 4-cycle aggregation behave similarly on several datasets, but the discrete expressivity question is open in this formalization.

If no pair is found up to seven vertices, feed larger graph6 files, preferably generated by `geng` from nauty:

```bash
geng -c 8 > graphs_n8.g6
python experiments/search_pairs.py --source graph6 --graph6-file graphs_n8.g6 \
  --weaker tri_square1 --stronger 2wl --out results/tri_square1_vs_2wl_n8.jsonl
```

For large searches, shard by graph6 file and run multiple processes.  This proof-search task is mostly CPU-bound and memory-bound; an H100 is useful only if the refinement kernels are later rewritten in PyTorch/JAX.

python experiments/search_pairs.py --source atlas --max-n 7 \

A hit proves strictness of one-type over two-type.

### 7.4 Search for structural witnesses

For every found pair, compute small homomorphism-count differences by brute force using `motifwl.hom.hom_count`.  Prioritize patterns of treewidth two: cycles, theta graphs, ladders, and small series-parallel graphs.  A useful witness should separate the pair under 2-WL while being absent from the current generated class, suggesting the missing constructor.

## 8. What would count as a major result

The most valuable outcomes are:

1. **Strictness theorem.**  Find and prove a graph-pair family with
   \[
   G_n\equiv_{\Delta+\square_1}H_n
   \quad\text{but}\quad
   G_n\not\equiv_{2\mathrm{WL}}H_n.
   \]
   This would place triangle-plus-4-cycle EB-WL strictly between EB-1WL and 2-WL.

2. **Equality theorem.**  Prove
   \[
   \mathcal C_{\Delta\square_1}=\{F:\operatorname{tw}(F)\le2\}.
   \]
   This would show that an edge-only refinement using triangles and 4-cycles reaches full 2-WL homomorphism expressivity while retaining an edge-state space.

3. **One-type/two-type theorem.**  Prove or refute
   \[
   \Delta+\square_2\equiv\Delta+\square_1.
   \]
   A strictness proof would justify the more expressive one-type triple aggregation.  An equivalence proof would justify the cheaper two-type design.

The first two are the major open conclusion of this proof program.
